How the calculation works

Basic health insurance (KVG/LAMal) splits every calendar year into three stretches:

StretchWho paysUntil
1. FranchiseYou pay 100% of the covered billsthe bills add up to your Franchise: CHF 300, 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000 or 2,500 for adults; CHF 0 to 600 for children
2. Selbstbehalt (co-payment)You pay 10%, the insurer 90%your 10% adds up to CHF 700 in the year (CHF 350 for a child)
3. After bothThe insurer pays 100%31 December; on 1 January everything starts again at zero

So the most an adult with the CHF 300 Franchise pays in a year is CHF 1,000, and with the CHF 2,500 Franchise it is CHF 3,200, premiums not counted.

Example

Marc has the CHF 300 Franchise and five bills this year: 210.00, 164.80, 540.00, 122.60 and 432.00, together CHF 1,469.40. The first CHF 300 are his. Of the remaining 1,169.40 he pays 10%, CHF 116.94. His share is CHF 416.94 and the insurer's is CHF 1,052.46. The insurer has paid back CHF 553.32 so far, so CHF 499.14 is still owed to him: part of it for a bill he sent in and has no statement for, the rest for a bill he never sent. Five things people get wrong

A bill only counts once the insurer has seen it

If you pay the doctor yourself and keep the bill because "it is below the Franchise anyway", the insurer's count of your Franchise stays at zero. Send every bill in, even the small ones in January. Otherwise the expensive bill in October is charged against a Franchise you had in fact already used up.

The date of treatment decides the year, not the date of the bill

A treatment on 20 December counts toward this year's Franchise even if the bill arrives in February. A treatment on 3 January starts the new year's count.

You have time, but not for ever

The claim against the insurer lapses five years after the end of the month the treatment took place in. A bill found in a drawer two years later can still be sent in.

Some costs sit outside the count

A hospital stay costs CHF 15 a day on top (not for children, not for young adults in education, not for maternity). For a brand-name medicine where a cheaper generic exists, the Selbstbehalt is 40% instead of 10%. Maternity care from week 13 of pregnancy to eight weeks after the birth is free of Franchise and Selbstbehalt. This calculator leaves these three out.

Supplementary insurance is a different pot

Glasses, dental hygiene, the gym: these are never basic insurance. They do not use up the Franchise, and whether anything comes back depends on a supplementary policy (VVG), if you have one. Untick them above.

Should I change my Franchise for next year?

You can ask for a higher Franchise until 31 December and a lower one until 30 November, both for 1 January. As a rule of thumb: if your covered bills stay under about CHF 2,000 a year, the CHF 2,500 Franchise is cheaper overall because of the lower premium; above that, the CHF 300 one is. The middle steps rarely win. Compare your own premiums for both on priminfo.admin.ch, the federal comparison without adverts.
