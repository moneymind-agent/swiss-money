Leaving a flat in Switzerland: getting the deposit back, and the hand-over checklist

Three months' rent sit in a blocked account, and between you and that money stands one appointment: the hand-over (Wohnungsabgabe, état des lieux de sortie). This page gives you the dates that count, what a landlord may charge you for a damaged item of a given age, and a checklist from the notice letter to the day the deposit is paid out.

The deposit: what the law says

Art. 257e of the Code of Obligations sets four rules for a flat. The deposit is at most three months' rent. It has to be in a bank account or deposit in your name, not in the landlord's own account. The bank pays it out only with both signatures, or on a payment order or court judgment that has become final. And if the landlord has started no legal action against you within one year after the end of the lease, you can ask the bank for the money without him. The interest is yours.

So a landlord cannot simply "keep" the deposit. He needs your signature under a statement of what is deducted, or he has to go to the conciliation authority. If you agree to a deduction, sign for that amount and ask for the rest to be released at once.

Normal wear, damage, and age

Normal wear: you owe nothingDamage: you owe the remaining value
Faded or yellowed walls after years of living; marks where furniture and pictures were; properly filled dowel holes in a reasonable number; traffic marks on parquet and carpet; limescale that cleaning removesWalls yellowed by smoking; children's drawings on the wallpaper; burn marks, deep scratches or water stains on a floor; a cracked basin or hob; broken blinds; a missing key (then often the lock as well)

For damage, age counts. If wall paint is expected to last eight years and was six years old, a quarter of the repainting cost is yours and three quarters would have been the landlord's anyway. Once an item is older than its expected life you owe nothing for it, even if you damaged it. The landlord has to show the age if he says the item is newer than you think. Typical lives in the joint table: wall paint 8 years, carpet 10, sealing of parquet 10, a fridge 10, dishwasher, oven and washing machine 15. Liability insurance (Privathaftpflicht) pays damage to a rented flat, not wear and not gradual damage; report the damage before the hand-over.

The hand-over protocol

At the hand-over the landlord has to inspect the flat and tell you straight away which defects he holds you responsible for (Art. 267a CO). What he does not report then he can no longer claim, apart from defects that could not be seen in an ordinary inspection, which he has to report as soon as he finds them. This is why the protocol matters more than anything said afterwards: read every line, compare it with the move-in protocol, and write your objection beside a point you do not accept. Signing "seen" is not the same as signing "I pay".

Notice: the dates

For a flat the law sets a notice period of three months, to the dates customary in the place (often the end of March, June and September, in many places not the end of December); your contract may set a longer period or name other dates (Art. 266c CO). The notice has to be in writing, signed by every tenant on the lease, and for a family home with the written consent of your spouse or registered partner. It counts on the day the landlord receives it or could collect it from the post office, not the day you post it: a letter that arrives on the 1st is a month late.

If you want to leave earlier or between dates, you are released once you present a new tenant who can pay and whom the landlord can reasonably be expected to accept, ready to take the lease over on the same terms (Art. 264 CO). The landlord may take a few weeks to check. Without such a tenant you owe the rent until the next ordinary date.

If you do not agree

The conciliation authority for tenancy matters (Schlichtungsbehörde, autorité de conciliation) in your district is the first stop for every tenancy dispute and costs nothing. The tenants' association (Mieterinnen- und Mieterverband, ASLOCA) advises members and checks final statements.
