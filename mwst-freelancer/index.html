The common mistake is to take 8.1% of a gross amount: 8.1% of CHF 1,899 is 153.82, but the tax inside CHF 1,899 is 142.29. Claim the first figure as input tax and every receipt in your books is wrong by 8%.

5-Rappen rounding

The smallest Swiss coin is 5 Rappen, so totals are customarily rounded to CHF 0.05: 2,637.64 becomes 2,637.65, and 2,637.62 becomes 2,637.60. Round once, at the invoice total, never line by line and never the tax amount by itself. The VAT Act does not ask for rounding; an invoice to the Rappen paid by bank transfer is just as valid. What you report is the amount you actually charged, so a rounded total is the turnover including tax, and the few Rappen of difference disappear in the quarterly figures.

Do I have to charge MWST at all?

You must register once your turnover from taxable work reaches CHF 100,000 in a year, counting customers in Switzerland and abroad together. Below that you may register voluntarily, which pays off when your customers are companies (they reclaim the tax, and you reclaim yours on equipment and rent). If you are not registered, you write no MWST on the invoice and no rate: a tax amount shown on an invoice is owed to the tax administration, registered or not.

What an invoice must show

Under Art. 26 of the VAT Act, an invoice from a registered business carries:

  • your name and place as you use them in business, and your VAT number in the form CHE-123.456.789 MWST;
  • the customer's name and place;
  • the date or the period of the work, if it is not the invoice date;
  • what was supplied: kind, object and extent;
  • the price;
  • the tax rate and the tax amount; if the price includes the tax, the rate is enough ("incl. 8.1% MWST").

The rate follows the date the work was done, not the date of the invoice. Till receipts up to CHF 400 need not name the customer, which is why the supermarket slip for office coffee is good enough for your books. Keep invoices and receipts for ten years.

Reporting: every quarter, 60 days

With the ordinary (effective) method you report every quarter: the turnover, the tax on it, minus the input tax on what you bought for the business. The return and the payment are due 60 days after the quarter ends, so by the end of May, August, November and February, and since 2025 the return is filed online in the tax administration's ePortal. Since 2025 a business with up to CHF 5,005,000 of turnover can ask to report once a year instead, with instalments during the year.

The alternative for small businesses is the net tax rate method (Saldosteuersatz): you still invoice 8.1%, but pay the tax administration a flat percentage of your gross turnover set for your trade, report twice a year and claim no input tax. It is open up to CHF 5,024,000 of turnover and CHF 108,000 of tax a year and suits people with few expenses. Calculators 2 and 3 are for the effective method.

Input tax: what you can take back

The MWST on what you buy for the business comes off what you owe, if you have a receipt and the purchase serves your taxable work. A purchase used partly in private counts for its business share only: a phone plan of CHF 65 a month used 60% for work carries 4.87 of MWST, of which 2.92 is yours to claim. Nothing comes back on purchases for exempt work without an option (teaching, most health care, insurance) or on receipts with no tax on them, such as purchases abroad or from unregistered sellers.
