How the weighted average is calculated

Multiply each grade by the ECTS of its course, add the products, and divide by the ECTS of the graded courses. Courses without a grade yet, and courses that are only passed or failed, are in neither sum.

44 ÷ 9 = 4.89. The plain mean of the three grades would be 4.67: the two larger courses pull the weighted figure up. The French course is under 4.0, so 7 of the 9 credits are earned.

What grade do I need in the exams still open?

Take the target average times all ECTS, graded and open together, subtract the grade points you already have, and divide by the open ECTS. In the example, 18 ECTS are still open and the target is 5.0: (5.0 × 27 − 44) ÷ 18 = 5.06. That figure is itself an ECTS-weighted average over the open courses, so a 5.5 in a 6-credit course makes room for a 4.75 in another 6-credit course with the third at 5.0.

If the result is above 6, the target cannot be reached with these courses. If it is below 4, the target is safe as far as the average goes, but each course still has to reach its pass mark to give you its credits.

What your university may do differently

The calculation above is the plain ECTS-weighted mean, which is what most Swiss study regulations describe. The details are set by each faculty, and they are where two students with the same grades end up with different figures:

  • Rounding. Course grades come in half or quarter steps at some universities and in tenths at others; the final average is rounded to one or two decimals, or to a quarter or half grade, depending on the regulation. This page shows two decimals, rounded half up.
  • Failed and repeated courses. Whether a failed attempt stays in the average, is replaced by the retake, or is left out differs. Enter here what your transcript of records counts.
  • Compensation. Some programmes let an insufficient grade inside a module group be compensated by better ones, so credits are awarded for the group; others require every course to be passed on its own.
  • Other weights. A thesis or a final exam can count with a weight that is not its ECTS, and some programmes compute the degree grade from module-group grades, not from single courses.

The document that decides is the study regulation of your programme (Studienreglement, Prüfungsordnung, règlement d'études) and the transcript your faculty issues. If its figure differs from this one, its figure is the one that counts.

ECTS in three lines

A full-time year is 60 ECTS, so a semester is 30; one credit stands for about 25 to 30 hours of work, lectures and self-study together. A bachelor's degree is 180 ECTS, a master's 90 or 120. Credits are awarded only when the course is passed, and then in full: a 4.0 and a 6.0 earn the same credits and differ only in the average.

Swiss grades on the German scale

German universities usually convert foreign grades with the modified Bavarian formula: 1 + 3 × (best grade − your grade) ÷ (best grade − lowest pass grade). With the Swiss 6 and 4 that is 1 + 3 × (6 − average) ÷ 2, so 4.89 becomes 2.7, a 6.0 becomes 1.0 and a 4.0 becomes 4.0. The calculator shows it for your average. The receiving university does the official conversion and may use its own table.
