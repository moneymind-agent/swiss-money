Swiss tax return: which documents to collect, and which deductions to check

The tax return itself is an afternoon. What takes weeks is the paper: a statement that only arrives in February, a receipt nobody kept, a deduction you did not know you had. Tick what applies to you and get your own list of documents, with who sends each one and when, and the deductions worth checking before you file.

The deductions to check, with the federal figures

You file one return, but it is assessed twice: once for the direct federal tax and once for canton and commune. The federal figures below are the same everywhere in Switzerland and are unchanged from tax year 2025 to 2026. Cantons set their own amounts for most lines, often higher; the canton's guide (Wegleitung) that comes with the form lists them.

Five things that cost people money

Taking the flat rate where the real cost is higher, or the reverse

The flat 3% for other work costs needs no receipt and is almost always the better choice. Commuting and training are the opposite: there is no flat amount, and without the pass receipt or the invoice the line is empty.

Not declaring securities because "they made nothing"

35% withholding tax is taken off every Swiss dividend and off larger interest payments. It comes back only through the list of securities in your return. Leave the account out and the 35% stays with the state; the tax statement from your bank lists the amounts to reclaim.

Medical bills thrown away because of the 5%

On a net income of CHF 90,000 the first CHF 4,500 count for nothing. One crown, one pair of glasses and a year of Franchise and co-payment get there faster than people think, and several cantons use a lower threshold than the Confederation. Keep the bills until you have added them up; the box above does it.

A 3a payment that arrived in January

Only what was booked on the 3a account by 31 December counts for that year. The certificate shows the booking date; if it shows less than you sent, the last payment arrived late and belongs in next year's return.

Asking for the extension after the deadline

The date is printed on the form your canton sends in January or February; in many cantons it is 31 March. An extension is a short online request and is granted almost everywhere, in some cantons for a fee, as long as you ask before the date passes. After it you get a reminder, then a fee, then an assessment by estimate.

If tax is taken from your salary (Quellensteuer)

With a B or L permit and tax at source you do not file a return unless you earn more than CHF 120,000 gross a year, own assets or have other income that requires it, or ask for one. Deductions that the source tax tariff does not include (pillar 3a, a pension fund buy-in, training, high medical bills) reach you only if you ask for an ordinary assessment at your cantonal tax office by 31 March of the following year. In most cantons that choice then holds for the following years as well.
