Säule 3a 2026: how much can I still pay in, and what does it save?

The 3a deduction is the one tax saving almost everyone with a Swiss income can take, and it expires on 31 December: what you have not paid in by then is gone for this tax year. The figures for 2026

Your situationMaximum 3a payment in 2026
With a pension fundCHF 7,258
Without a pension fund20% of net income from work, at most CHF 36,288

The amounts are per person, not per household: a couple who both work can each pay in the maximum. You need an income from work that is subject to AHV; a year without one is a year without 3a. The maximum is the same whether you work full time or part time, and you can spread it over several 3a accounts as long as the total stays under the limit.

What it saves

The payment is deducted from your taxable income, so the saving is the payment multiplied by your marginal tax rate. CHF 7,258 at a marginal rate of 25% is about CHF 1,815 less tax; at 35% about CHF 2,540. The money is not a gift: it is locked until five years before the ordinary retirement age (earlier for buying a home you live in, going self-employed or leaving Switzerland for good), and it is taxed once when you withdraw it, at a reduced rate and separately from your other income.

When the money has to leave your account

What counts is the day the payment arrives in the 3a account, not the day you send it. It has to be booked in 2026. Banks and 3a apps publish a cut-off for year-end payments, usually a few working days before Christmas, and a payment that arrives in January counts for 2027. Send it by mid-December and check a few days later that it is on the 3a statement. If you pay monthly by standing order, check that the twelve payments really add up to the maximum: after the limit was raised, many old standing orders still run on the earlier amount (CHF 588 a month gave the 2024 limit of 7,056; CHF 604.83 gives 7,258).

New since 2026: paying in for a year you missed

Until now a missed year was lost. From 2026 you can make up gaps, but only gaps from 2025 onward, and with conditions: you had an AHV income in Switzerland in the year of the gap and have one now, you first pay this year's full maximum, and the buy-in for past years is limited to the small maximum (CHF 7,258) per year on top. A gap can be filled for up to ten years afterwards. The buy-in is deductible like the ordinary payment. Ask your 3a provider for its buy-in form: it wants a declaration of what you paid in the gap year.

If tax is withheld from your salary (Quellensteuer)

With a B or L permit and tax taken at source, the 3a deduction does not happen by itself. You get it by asking for an ordinary assessment or a correction of the withholding tax, at your cantonal tax office, by 31 March of the following year. That date is strict, and in most cantons a request for an ordinary assessment applies to the following years too. Above a gross salary of CHF 120,000 you are assessed ordinarily anyway.

Is one account enough?

A 3a account has to be withdrawn in one piece. Because the withdrawal tax rises with the amount, people who will have a larger balance open several accounts over the years (three to five is common) and withdraw them in different tax years. The yearly maximum stays the same however many accounts you have.
